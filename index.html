How it handles every inquiry

Four doors in. Two ways out.

One gate in the middle, and a loop back to the campaign. An inquiry is captured and routed, never assessed.

1

Disclose and consent

It says it is automated, takes recording consent, and offers the emergency bypass, before anything else happens.

2

Understand the intent

Booking, reschedule, a price or policy question, a prescription refill request, directions, or a worry about an animal.

3

The clinical gate

Any mention of a symptom, condition, injury, medication or "is this serious?" stops here. It captures species, age, sign and duration, states no opinion, and hands off.

The situation

Two revenue channels, both leaking, neither measured.

An emergency hospital has two customers and they behave nothing alike. One is a frightened family at 2am who will drive to whichever hospital they find first. The other is a general-practice veterinarian deciding, during office hours, where to send a case they cannot handle.

Copper Creek was marketing to neither. Their site was built for consumers, so the referral audience had nowhere to go. Their overnight phone rolled to a service that took a message. And nobody had noticed that fourteen practices who used to refer monthly had not sent a case in over a year.

The audit's blunt finding: the hospital did not have an awareness problem. It had a 2am answering problem and a lapsed-referrer problem, and the second one was worth more.

What we did

Fixed the phone. Then rebuilt the referral channel.

Weeks 1–4

Overnight intake

Call tracking on every number, then a call-handling script and staffing change so the overnight line reached a trained team member rather than a message service. Recovery texts on every abandoned call within sixty seconds.

Weeks 3–8

A door for referring vets

A referral portal separate from the consumer site: direct lines to the on-duty clinician, case-status updates back to the referrer, and discharge summaries that arrive the same day. Then a lapsed-referrer campaign to the fourteen practices who had gone quiet.

Weeks 6–10

Found at 2am

Hours, on-premises status and services corrected across every listing. Florida and several other states require emergency advertising to state the hours covered and whether a veterinarian is on premises or on call, so the listings had to say so too. Overnight paid search targeted to the drive-time radius only.

Ongoing
